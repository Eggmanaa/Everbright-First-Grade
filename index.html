The Grand Narrative

A Darkness Falls Upon the Stars

Deep within the Castle Observatory, Copernicus discovers an ancient star map painted across the ceiling. Most of its constellations have gone dark. Stellara the Star Keeper awakens to reveal the truth: Everbright was once connected to great civilizations across the world through a network of starlight paths.

A mysterious darkness called The Dimming is erasing these connections one by one. A creature called the Shadow Moth feeds on forgotten knowledge, growing larger as civilizations are forgotten.

Princess Rosie must journey through time and space to restore each starlight path, collecting constellation fragments along the way. Each journey teaches her the knowledge of that civilization, that science, that era of history.

But the Shadow Moth is not truly evil — it is lost. Once a beautiful Starlight Butterfly, it was corrupted by ignorance. As Rosie restores knowledge to the world, the moth shrinks, and by the final mission, it transforms back into its true form.

Over 15 missions spanning 180 school days, Rosie travels to ancient Mesopotamia, Egypt, the Americas, colonial settlements, and beyond — learning reading, math, science, and history at every stop.

"The Star Map was never really lost — it was waiting for someone brave and curious enough to learn everything needed to read it."

For the Game Master

How to Play

The 11+1 Day Cycle

Days 1–11: Quest Days

Each day, complete 4 assignments (one per subject). Completing all four earns that day's unique quest item.

Language Arts (CKLA Skills) — Every day
Listening (CKLA Knowledge) — Every day
Math (CKMath) — Every day
Science (CKSci) — Alternating days
Social Studies (CKHG) — Alternating days

Day 12: Mission Day

A special culminating event. The child uses all 11 collected quest items to complete the major mission. No formal assignments — the mission itself is the assessment.

Missing items? If the child didn't earn all 11 items, the mission can't fully succeed. This creates a natural consequence that motivates daily engagement across all subjects.

A Typical Quest Day

Morning Block

Language Arts — Phonics/decoding, spelling, decodable reading, writing, grammar

Listening — Read-aloud from the current domain, discussion, vocabulary, oral retelling

Afternoon Block

Math — Lesson, practice, story problems or hands-on activity

Science or Social Studies — Whichever subject is scheduled for that day

The Star Navigator's Pack

A visual inventory on the website. Each day's earned item appears in the pack. Over 11 quest days, the child collects 11 unique items — all needed for Mission Day.

Items are thematic, not abstract. Instead of coins, the child earns tools tied to the narrative: a Star Compass, a Clay Tablet Kit, a Hieroglyphic Key. This creates intrinsic motivation — they're building their toolkit for the adventure ahead.

The Star Navigator's Journal

A physical or digital artifact where the child records findings from each mission:

  • ★ Observation Pages — Drawings and notes from each civilization
  • ★ Vocabulary Scrolls — New words organized by domain
  • ★ Math Logs — Problems solved with work shown
  • ★ Letter Pages — Letters to civilization guides
  • ★ Constellation Tracker — Star chart that fills in as missions complete
